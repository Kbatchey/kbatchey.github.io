I am a PhD candidate in Plant Breeding at the University of Florida, based at the Gulf Coast Research and Education Center in Wimauma, Florida. My research focuses on genetic mapping and disease resistance breeding in tomato and pepper, combining field trials, molecular markers, and data analysis to develop improved cultivars.

I hold an MBA in Business Management from the University of São Paulo (2024) and a B.S. in Agricultural Sciences from EARTH University, Costa Rica (2021). I speak Ewe, French, English, and Spanish, and intermediate Portuguese.

Research

Resistance to target spot in tomato

Evaluating foliar and fruit responses of tomato breeding lines to Corynespora cassiicola, the cause of target spot, to identify resistant lines for cultivar development, and validating candidate genes with CRISPR/Cas9.

ASHS 2026, Tomato Breeders Roundtable 2026, Plant Science Symposium

Genetic mapping of internal white tissue

Mapping the genetic basis of internal white tissue, a fruit quality disorder in tomato, and developing molecular markers to support selection.

Tomato Field Day 2025, Plant Science Symposium 2025

Field trials and marker-assisted breeding

Designing replicated field trials (60–70 genotypes per season) and running DNA extraction, PCR, and HRM genotyping on over 1,000 samples per year, including work on Xanthomonas resistance in tomato and pepper.
